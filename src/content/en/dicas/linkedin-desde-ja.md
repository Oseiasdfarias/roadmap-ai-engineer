## What it is

LinkedIn is a professional social network where companies post jobs and recruiters look for people. Your profile is a public page with a headline, summary, experience, projects and skills. Building it early, before you need it, leaves your track record ready on the day you apply for your first role.

## Why it matters

Recruiters usually search for candidates by terms such as "Python", "internship" or "data" and tend to read profiles quickly. LinkedIn is also where you apply through LinkedIn Jobs. A profile without a clear headline or projects is less likely to show up in those searches, even if you have studied a lot. The article in the references details each section of the profile.

A good profile does not guarantee replies. It keeps missing information from counting against you.

## Step by step

1. **Use a simple, clear photo**, with your face visible, and pick a neutral cover image.
2. **Write the headline with what you do and study**, not just "student". Include the field and one or two technologies.
3. **Write the About section in four to six lines.** Say what you study, what you have built and what kind of role you are looking for. Write in the first person, without empty adjectives.
4. **Put your projects in Featured**, with the GitHub link and one line about the result.
5. **Fill in education and courses**, with dates. In skills, list only what you can defend in a conversation.
6. **Use words from the postings.** Open three openings in the field and use the same terms where true.
7. **Post what you learn**, once a week or every two weeks: a short text about a bug you solved or a project you finished. It builds a track record and attracts people from the field.
8. **Follow companies and people in the field** and turn on job alerts (see the tip Where to look for openings).

## Example

Headline, before:

> Student

After:

> Tech student | Learning Python and SQL | First GitHub project in progress

At this stage, the headline says what you are learning, without pretending to have experience you do not have yet.

About section sentence, before:

> Passionate about technology, looking for an opportunity to grow.

After:

> I am learning Python and SQL and building, on GitHub, a program that organizes an expense spreadsheet (still in progress, with a README and simple tests). I am looking for an internship in development or data, to learn by working in a team.

The second version says what you are doing, is honest about the project's stage and uses terms that searches find. When your first AI project is ready, update the text.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving the headline generic**, such as "Student" or "Looking for opportunities".
- **Listing fifty skills.** It loses credibility and dilutes the ones that matter.
- **Copying ready-made AI texts without reviewing them.** Your profile ends up like everyone else's.
- **Posting to get attention**, with motivational quotes. Prefer what you learned and showed working.
- **Ignoring messages and invitations.** Reply politely, even to decline.
- **Publishing company or personal data.** Never expose confidential information.

## Checklist

- Simple photo and cover.
- Headline with field and technologies.
- About section of four to six lines with what you built and what you are looking for.
- Projects in Featured with links.
- Skills you can defend.
- Terms from three real postings applied where true.
- One short post published.
- Job alerts set up.
