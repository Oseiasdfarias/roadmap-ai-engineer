## What it is

Conversational English is the ability to speak and understand in real time: meetings, interviews and the team's daily conversations. It comes after technical reading (see the tip English: technical reading) and is the hardest part for non-native speakers, because it requires answering without time to look anything up.

## Why it matters

Companies with teams in several countries may run part of the interviews and meetings in English, and in those cases speaking clearly counts in the evaluation. Besides, much of the technical discussion and many events in the AI field happen in English. Check in the postings of the companies you want whether spoken English is required.

Fluency is not reached in weeks, and there is no guaranteed timeline. What works is frequency: a little, every week, for months.

## Step by step

1. **Find out your current level.** Record two minutes talking about your work and listen back. Write down what tripped you up: vocabulary, verb tense or pronunciation.
2. **Set fixed practice times**, using BBC Learning English material as your listening base, for example three 30-minute sessions a week, with dates on the calendar.
3. **Talk to other people.** Private lessons, conversation groups, language exchange partners or study partners. Talking alone helps, but it does not replace hearing an answer.
4. **Prepare the vocabulary of your field** (the BBC English at Work section has dialogues from work situations): describing a project, explaining a decision, asking for clarification ("Could you clarify...?").
5. **Rehearse the interview in English.** Write your five stories (see Behavioral interview) in English, and tell them out loud, with a timer.
6. **Watch and repeat (shadowing).** Pick a technical talk, listen to a sentence and repeat it, imitating the rhythm.
7. **Measure every month.** Record the same text and compare. Note what improved and what is still hard.
8. **Accept making mistakes.** Correcting everything on the spot freezes your speech. Write down the mistakes and fix them later.

## Example

Answer to "Tell me about your project", before:

> My project is about a chatbot. It uses AI.

After (illustrative example):

> I built a search tool for technical manuals. The problem was that technicians wasted time looking for fault codes in long PDFs. I combined keyword search and semantic search, because codes don't have meaning for the model. In my tests, the right page appeared in the top five results in 76% of the questions.

The script is problem, decision, result (illustrative numbers, use yours). The answer uses short sentences and vocabulary you already studied in technical reading.

## Common mistakes

- **Studying only grammar.** Speaking requires speaking practice.
- **Waiting until you feel ready to speak.** Start with simple sentences.
- **Practicing once a month, in long blocks.** Frequency beats duration.
- **Translating in your head from your native language.** Learn whole sentences from your field.
- **Avoiding interviews in English.** They are practice too.
- **Not recording yourself.** Without listening, you do not notice what needs to improve.

## Checklist

- Initial recording made and listened to.
- Three weekly sessions scheduled.
- At least one conversation with another person per week.
- Five interview stories written in English.
- Phrases for asking for clarification and saying "I don't know" memorized.
- Monthly recording to compare progress.
- One mock interview in English done.
