## What it is

It is the map of places where tech internships are posted, and a method so you do not depend on luck. The Brazilian channels below are the ones this roadmap was written around; in other countries, look for the local equivalents (national job boards, university career centers, internship programs). Internships with "AI" in the title are usually rare. That is why the search includes nearby areas that give real team experience: development, data, systems support and software automation.

## Why it matters

People who search in a single channel see only part of the openings. Each channel has its audience: many companies use recruiting platforms, internship programs may have fixed dates, and some openings circulate by referral. Knowing the channels and setting up alerts saves hours and keeps you from missing deadlines.

No channel guarantees a reply. The goal is to increase the number of good applications, not to find the ideal opening.

## Step by step

1. **LinkedIn Jobs.** Search for "internship", "intern", "developer" and "data". Filter by location (or remote), level and posting date. Create one alert per search to get new openings by email.
2. **Gupy (Brazil).** Many mid-sized and large Brazilian companies receive applications through the Gupy portal. Create your profile once, keep it complete and favorite the companies you care about.
3. **CIEE (Brazil).** The Company-School Integration Center brokers internships. Sign up and keep your college details up to date.
4. **Nube (Brazil).** An internship and trainee platform. Sign up and use the area filters.
5. **Company career pages.** List ten companies you admire, find each one's "Careers" page and save the link. Internship programs have opening dates, usually announced on the company's social channels.
6. **Communities.** Study groups, servers and events in the field (see Network and referrals) share openings and give referrals.
7. **Accept nearby areas.** Web development, data, systems support and industrial automation with software give team experience, reviewed code and deadlines, which count in the next step.
8. **Set a routine.** Twenty minutes a day to check alerts, plus one hour a week to save openings and tailor applications.

## Example

Alert, before:

> Search: "artificial intelligence"

After (illustrative):

> Search: "Python internship" OR "development internship" OR "data internship". Location: your city and remote. Frequency: daily.

The first alert brings almost no results, because internships with that name are rare. The second covers openings where you use Python every day, and that counts as experience.

## Common mistakes

- **Searching only for "AI".** The search comes back empty and you get discouraged.
- **Using a single channel.** Openings are spread across platforms and company pages.
- **Leaving your profile incomplete** on the platforms you use. Incomplete profiles can be left out of filters and recruiter searches.
- **Ignoring the internship's terms and conditions.** Check the contract, the hours and whether the role requires college enrollment.
- **Applying without reading the posting.** See Focused applications.
- **Falling for a scam.** Be suspicious of openings that charge a fee or ask for bank details before the hiring process.

## Checklist

- Complete profiles on LinkedIn and on the job platforms of your country.
- At least three job alerts on LinkedIn.
- A list of ten companies with their careers link saved.
- One community in the field followed.
- Nearby areas accepted in the search.
- A fixed weekly time slot to review openings.
