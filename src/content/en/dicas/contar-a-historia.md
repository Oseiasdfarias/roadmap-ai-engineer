## What it is

Telling your story is describing each project, and your path, in four parts: the problem, the technical decision you made, the measured result and what you learned. It is the same script for LinkedIn, your résumé, the README and the interview. People changing fields need it, because the recurring question is: why you, now, for this role?

## Why it matters

Someone moving from software development to AI, or from another field into tech, has a past that seems not to fit. The script shows the line connecting what you did to what you do today. Without it, the résumé becomes a list of technologies, and the interview becomes a series of loose answers.

It also helps you stay truthful. A script with a measurement and a lesson needs no exaggeration: every claim has backing, and the interviewer can check it.

## Step by step

1. **List your three to five strongest projects**, including work, internship and personal projects.
2. **Write each one in four sentences**: the problem, the decision, the measurement and the lesson. One sentence per part, no adjectives.
3. **Look for the common thread.** What do the stories have in common? For example: "I like turning manual processes into measured systems". Use that as your opening line.
4. **Write the transition paragraph.** Two or three sentences saying where you came from, what you did in AI and where you want to go.
5. **Adapt it to the format**: one line in the résumé, one paragraph on LinkedIn, two minutes in the interview, one section in the README.
6. **Check the numbers.** Each measurement needs a source (README, dashboard, eval report; evals are tests of answer quality, and the article in the references explains how to start).
7. **Rehearse out loud** and ask someone to interrupt with "why?" and "how did you measure it?".

## Example

Answer to "Why are you moving into AI?", before:

> I have always liked technology and AI is the future.

After (illustrative numbers):

> For the last two years I worked on backend in Python. At the company, I proposed an assistant that suggests answers for support, citing the source article. In the pilot with the team, I measured time per ticket and the rate of accepted suggestions, and the average time dropped from 30 to 20 minutes. I learned that the hard part was not the model, but the data and the evaluation. I want to work on that full time.

The same text reduced to one résumé line (illustrative numbers, use yours; the pilot is modest on purpose):

> Built a pilot support assistant with an LLM (language model) and knowledge base search (Python, PostgreSQL). Reduced average time per ticket from 30 to 20 minutes in the team pilot.

The story has a problem, decision, measurement and lesson, and it works in different formats.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting with the technology.** Start with the problem.
- **Telling only the success.** Include a decision that went wrong and what changed, which builds credibility.
- **Inflating numbers.** You will be asked about them.
- **Using different versions in each place.** LinkedIn says one thing, the résumé another and the interview a third.
- **Hiding the career change.** Explain it briefly and positively, without apologizing.
- **Memorizing a script word for word.** Memorize the structure, not the sentences.

## Checklist

- Three to five projects written as problem, decision, measurement and lesson.
- Common thread defined in one sentence.
- Transition paragraph ready.
- One-line, one-paragraph and two-minute versions.
- Numbers with a checked source.
- LinkedIn, résumé and README tell the same story.
- Out-loud rehearsal done with deep-dive questions.
