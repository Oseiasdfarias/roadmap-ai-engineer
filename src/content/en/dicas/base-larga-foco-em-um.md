## What it is

A T-shaped profile is the idea of having a broad base (the horizontal bar of the T) and one area where you go deep (the vertical bar). In practice, for someone who will work with AI: knowing enough frontend, backend, databases, cloud and AI to ship a feature end to end, and choosing one of those areas to become a reference in.

## Why it matters

In small and mid-sized companies, teams are usually lean, and people who solve problems across several layers (the interface, the API, the database and the LLM part) have more room. In larger companies, roles tend to be more separated, and specialization weighs more.

This is a trend seen in job postings, not a rule, and there is no single source that proves it. Read the postings for the field and the company you want and adjust your path to them. The roadmap.sh reference lists what a full stack path usually includes, so you can build your horizontal bar.

## Step by step

1. **List the areas of the horizontal bar**: frontend, backend, databases, deployment and AI. Rate from 0 to 3 how well you know each one.
2. **Make sure you have the minimum in all of them.** For each area, can you build and debug a small part on your own? If not, plan a short project.
3. **Choose the area to go deep in.** Think about what you enjoy doing for hours and what the openings you care about ask for. It can be backend with AI, data, infrastructure or frontend.
4. **Keep the choice for a while** (a few months, for example) before revisiting it, so you do not change direction with every novelty.
5. **Go deep with real projects.** For backend, for example: authentication, queues, tests and observability. Write down what you learned.
6. **Show both bars in your résumé and interviews.** Highlight the deep area, and at the same time show that you ship end to end.
7. **Review at the end of each year.** The market changes, and it is fine to adjust the area.

## Example

LinkedIn summary line, before:

> Junior developer who likes everything: frontend, backend, AI, data and cloud.

After:

> Junior developer focused on backend and applied AI. I build Python APIs (FastAPI, PostgreSQL) and integrate them with LLMs (language models), with a simple React interface when needed.

The second version says where your focus is and shows that you also reach the other layers.

Self-assessment, illustrative:

| Area | Level (0 to 3) |
|---|---|
| Backend | 2, going deep |
| Databases | 2 |
| Frontend | 1 |
| AI | 2 |
| Deployment | 1 |

## Common mistakes

- **Staying shallow in everything.** Without any strong area, you look interchangeable.
- **Going deep without the base.** An AI specialist who cannot test an API struggles in small teams.
- **Changing focus with every trend.**
- **Putting off the choice forever.** Pick an area, even if you revisit it later.
- **Thinking a broad base replaces depth.** Deep-dive interviews show the difference.
- **Treating the profile as fixed.** It evolves with experience and with what the market asks for.

## Checklist

- Self-assessment done for the five areas.
- At least one small project in each base area.
- Deep area chosen and written down.
- A project that shows the chosen area in depth.
- Résumé and LinkedIn show both focus and breadth.
- Profile review scheduled for a few months from now.
