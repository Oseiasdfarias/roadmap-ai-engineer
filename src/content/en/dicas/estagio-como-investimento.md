## What it is

An internship is a supervised period of teamwork with a learning commitment. On your path to AI Engineering, it is an investment: beyond the stipend, you gain the experience of working with other people, which no course fully teaches.

In Brazil, internships have their own rules, set by the Internship Law (Law 11,788/2008); other countries have their own rules. This tip is not legal advice: read your contract and, if in doubt, ask your college's internship office or an organization such as CIEE (see the references).

## Why it matters

Many junior roles ask for team experience: code reviewed by other people, tests, on-time delivery and communication. A personal project shows that you can build. An internship shows that you build with other people, and it goes into your résumé as experience.

That is why, in this phase, it is worth comparing roles by what you would learn, not only by the stipend. But learning is no excuse to accept any condition.

## Step by step

1. **Confirm the minimum before accepting.** These items are requirements, not negotiation: a stipend agreed in writing, hours compatible with your classes, an internship agreement signed by you, the company and the college, and active enrollment. If the role asks you to work full time like an employee, without registration and with a low stipend, be suspicious: that is a job disguised as an internship.
2. **Assess the learning.** Ask who will supervise you, what technologies they use and whether there is code review.
3. **Agree on goals in the first weeks.** Ask what a good result looks like at 30, 60 and 90 days.
4. **Ask for small tasks and deliver them.** One complete delivery is worth more than many started tasks.
5. **Learn the team's flow**: opening a pull request (a request for code review), receiving review, running tests, deploying.
6. **Document what you did.** Keep a list with date, problem, what you did and the result. It becomes résumé lines and interview stories (see Behavioral interview).
7. **Ask for feedback every two or three weeks**, in a specific way.
8. **Build your network and respect confidentiality.** Keep in touch with colleagues when the internship ends, but do not take company data, code or documents into your portfolio.

## Example

Asking for feedback, before:

> Hey, how am I doing?

After:

> Can I book 15 minutes this week? I would like to know how you rate my pull requests so far and what I could improve before the next delivery.

Résumé line, before:

> IT intern. Helped the team with various tasks.

After (illustrative numbers, use yours):

> Development intern. Implemented 6 endpoints of an internal Python API, wrote 18 automated tests for them and fixed 4 bugs reported by support.

The second line tells what you delivered, using measures an intern controls, without revealing company data.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing only by the stipend**, or ignoring it completely. Learning and fair conditions go together.
- **Accepting without an internship agreement** or with hours that keep you from attending classes.
- **Waiting for someone to tell you what to do.** Propose, ask and bring solutions.
- **Being afraid to ask.** Ask after trying, describing what you have already done.
- **Not recording what you deliver.** In six months you forget, and the résumé becomes vague.
- **Exposing company code or data.** It can breach your contract and close doors.

## Checklist

- Stipend, hours and the college's role confirmed in writing.
- Internship agreement signed by all three parties before starting.
- 30, 60 and 90-day goals agreed.
- Delivery list updated every week.
- Feedback request booked every few weeks.
- No company data or code outside the permitted channels.
- Résumé lines written with what you delivered.
