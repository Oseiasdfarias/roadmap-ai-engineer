## What it is

Your network is the set of people who know your work and can speak well of it. A referral is when someone inside a company recommends you for a role. Many companies have referral programs, and a referrer is usually listened to more closely than an anonymous résumé. Building a network takes months and is done before you need it.

## Why it matters

At established companies, competition for openings is usually high, and many companies have referral programs. There is no reliable figure for how many openings are filled this way, and it varies by company. Someone who knows you can confirm what the résumé only claims: that you deliver, learn and work well with others.

A referral does not replace the hiring process. It opens the door, and you still need to pass the stages.

## Step by step

1. **Join communities in the field.** Python, data and AI groups, and events such as Python Brasil or local meetups and conferences. Start by listening, then ask, then answer.
2. **Contribute to open source.** Pick a project you use, read its contribution guide and start with documentation, a typo fix or an issue tagged "good first issue".
3. **Show what you do.** Post what you learn and your projects, with short texts on LinkedIn and GitHub.
4. **Talk to people, not just ask for jobs.** Ask for career advice, an opinion on a project or how the team works. Thank them and share the outcome.
5. **Help first.** Answer questions, share material, refer someone. A network is an exchange.
6. **Ask for a referral with context.** Only ask people who have seen your work. Send the posting link, your résumé and two sentences on why you are a good fit, and make it easy to say no.
7. **Keep in touch.** Update the people who helped, after the interview and after the result.

## Example

Referral request, before:

> Hi, are there openings at your company? Can you refer me?

After (illustrative names):

> Hi Carlos. I saw that the company's data team opened a mid-level AI Engineer role (link). Since you know my technical manual search project, I thought it made sense to ask: would you feel comfortable referring me? Here is my résumé and a paragraph on where my work fits the role. If it is not possible, no problem, and thank you for your time.

The second message has a specific role, a reference to what the person already knows about you and freedom to decline.

## Common mistakes

- **Looking for a network only when you lose your job.** Start earlier.
- **Asking for a referral from someone who has never seen your work.** They have nothing to say.
- **Sending a generic message to dozens of people.**
- **Asking for a job in the first conversation.**
- **Disappearing after getting help.**
- **Giving up on open source after one try.** Start small and stay consistent.

## Checklist

- One community followed and one active participation per month.
- One open source contribution submitted.
- Three advice conversations per quarter.
- A list of people who know your work.
- A referral request message template written and tested.
- Thanks and updates sent to the people who helped.
