## What it is

Technical reading in English is the ability to read documentation, articles and issues (problem discussions in repositories) without relying on translation, and to follow technical videos in the original language. It does not require speaking well or writing perfectly. It requires understanding the text of your field and keeping a routine. If English is your first language, use this tip for a second technical language or skip to the next one.

## Why it matters

Almost everything in AI appears first in English: model provider documentation, papers, new libraries and discussions. Translations, when they exist, arrive later and may be outdated. People who read the original learn earlier and make fewer mistakes because of a badly translated term.

Many roles, especially at larger companies, also ask for English. But reading comes first, and it is the skill you can practice on your own, for free and every day.

## Step by step

1. **Pick one fixed source**, such as the Claude documentation (the API introduction) or the Python tutorial, both in the references. Always reading the same source reduces the new vocabulary each day.
2. **Set a measurable goal.** For example, 20 minutes a day or one page of documentation a day, five days a week.
3. **Read first without translating.** Mark the words you did not understand and only look them up after finishing the paragraph, to try to get them from context.
4. **Keep your own vocabulary list.** A spreadsheet or notebook with the word, the sentence where it appeared and its meaning. Review it the following week.
5. **Watch technical videos in English**, first with English subtitles, then without subtitles.
6. **Write short things in English**: commit names, code comments and, when you feel ready, your project READMEs.
7. **Complement with BBC Learning English** if the obstacle is general English rather than technical: it helps with everyday vocabulary and listening.
8. **Review the goal every month.** If it got easy, switch to something harder, such as a research paper.

## Example

Example sentence, written for this exercise in the style of documentation (a paraphrase, not a quote):

> The model has no memory between requests, so you must send the full conversation history each time.

Step-by-step reading: "memory between requests" means the model keeps nothing from one call to the next. "You must send" means it is your job to include it. Full sentence: the model does not keep memory between requests, so you need to send the whole conversation history with every call.

Vocabulary entry, before:

> request

After:

> request: a call sent to an API. "The model has no memory between requests."

The second entry keeps the source sentence, and the sentence is what helps you remember later.

## Common mistakes

- **Translating every word.** You read slowly and lose the meaning of the whole.
- **Studying without a goal.** "When I have time" usually becomes never.
- **Switching sources every week.** You pay the cost of new vocabulary without building up.
- **Watching only videos with subtitles in your native language.** It does not train your ear or your reading in the original.
- **Waiting to speak well before you start reading.** They are separate skills, and reading pulls the rest along.
- **Trusting machine translation without checking technical terms**, which can come out wrong.

## Checklist

- One fixed source chosen.
- Daily or weekly goal written down, with a time slot.
- Your own vocabulary list created, with the source sentence.
- One piece of documentation read per week without a translator.
- One technical video per week with English subtitles.
- Monthly review of the goal and the source.
