## What it is

The résumé is a one-page document that shows, in a few seconds of reading, that you solve problems similar to the role's. For internships and first jobs in AI Engineering, projects are the center of the document. Education, courses and languages come in as support.

## Why it matters

In Brazil, many internship and tech openings go through recruiting platforms such as Gupy, and in many other countries through similar applicant tracking systems. They can read your résumé and compare the text with the job description before anyone opens it. After that screening, the recruiter reads quickly and looks for evidence, not adjectives.

A clear résumé does not guarantee an interview. It keeps you from being discarded for form, and it gives the interviewer concrete questions to ask.

## Step by step

1. **Read three to five postings for the role you want.** Write down the words that repeat, such as Python, SQL, APIs, RAG, Docker. Use the same terms when you actually have the experience. Write in the posting's language.
2. **Use a simple, single-column template.** Common section titles (Education, Projects, Experience, Skills), a standard font, no tables, icons or skill bars. Export to PDF with selectable text.
3. **Keep the header to one or two lines.** Name, city and state, email, phone, LinkedIn and GitHub. No photo, age, marital status or ID numbers: they do not help screening and they expose personal data.
4. **Write the projects section.** Two or three projects, each with a link to the repository and two or three lines in the problem, decision and measured result format.
5. **Include experience, even outside tech.** Teaching assistant roles, undergraduate research, volunteer work and jobs in another field count. Describe what you did and the result, not just the title.
6. **List skills you can defend in an interview.** If you cannot talk about it for five minutes, remove it.
7. **Review.** Read it out loud, ask someone else to read it and check that it fits on one page.

## Example

One project line, before:

> Developed a chatbot with Python, LangChain and generative AI to answer questions about documents.

The problem: it says which tools you used, but not what for, which decision you made or whether it worked.

The same line, after (illustrative numbers):

> Built a search with page citations over 12 public technical manuals (Python, PostgreSQL with pgvector). Replaced fixed-size text splitting with section-based splitting and raised search accuracy (recall@5) from 62% to 81% on 30 test questions.

Now the line has the problem (finding information in manuals), the decision (how to split the text) and the measured result. Recall@5 is the fraction of test questions where the right passage appears among the first five results. In your résumé, the numbers need to be yours and appear in the project's README (the repository's presentation file), because the interviewer will ask how you measured.

## Common mistakes

- **Listing technologies without context.** A line with ten tools says less than one project with a result.
- **Using a two-column layout, charts or text inside images.** Screening systems may read the wrong order or nothing at all.
- **Going over one page early in your career.** Cut what is not related to the role.
- **Writing a generic objective.** "Looking for an opportunity to grow" takes up space. If you use a summary at the top, say what you study and what you have already built.
- **Inventing or inflating results.** Any number in the résumé becomes an interview question.
- **Sending the same file to every role.** Adjust the order of the projects and the terms to the posting.

## Checklist

- Fits on one page, as a PDF with selectable text.
- Single-column layout, with no tables, icons, photo or personal data beyond contact details.
- LinkedIn and GitHub in the header, with working links.
- Two or three projects with links and lines in the problem, decision and result format.
- Every number has a source in the project's README.
- The role's terms appear where you actually have the experience.
- Written in the posting's language.
- Reviewed by someone else.
