## What it is

Focused applications means sending many applications without sending careless ones. You tailor the résumé to the posting, log each submission in a spreadsheet, track the answers and learn from the rejections. The opposite is mass-sending the same file, which wastes time and rarely gets a reply.

## Why it matters

For internships and first jobs, many people compete for each opening. Platforms such as Gupy (a hiring platform widely used in Brazil) and other applicant tracking systems can compare your résumé text with the posting text before a person opens the file (see Résumé). If the terms of the job do not appear where you have the experience, you may be discarded without being read.

Without a log, you forget where you applied, miss deadlines and cannot see what works. And without handling rejection, the search stops in the first month.

## Step by step

1. **Build a spreadsheet** with these columns: company, role, link, date sent, résumé version, channel, status and date of the next step.
2. **Read the whole posting.** Separate what the role requires from what is nice to have and check whether you meet most of the requirements.
3. **Tailor the résumé in ten to fifteen minutes.** Reorder the projects and adjust the terms to match the posting, only where true. Save the file with the role's name.
4. **Write a short message**, when there is a text field or a recruiter contact (if there is no contact, there is no need to push): who you are, why this role and one relevant project with a link.
5. **Log the submission** in the spreadsheet, with the date of the next step.
6. **Follow up.** If there is no answer within the posting's timeline, or in about a week, send a short, polite message.
7. **Treat rejection as data.** Log the reason when there is one and, every two weeks, look at the spreadsheet: which roles got a reply and what do they have in common?
8. **Keep a sustainable pace**, such as five well-made applications a week (an illustrative number; adjust to your time).

## Example

Follow-up, before:

> Hi, did you see my résumé?

After:

> Hi Marina (illustrative name). I applied for the development internship on the 12th and wanted to confirm my résumé arrived. I have a Python project for searching technical manuals that may be relevant to the team: github.com/user/project (illustrative link). Happy to talk. Thank you!

The new version says which role, when you applied, and gives the person a concrete reason to reply. Send it once, and do not repeat it every day.

## Common mistakes

- **Sending the same résumé everywhere.** You lose both the automated screening and the quick read.
- **Applying to roles you clearly do not meet.** Check what the posting requires. You do not need every nice-to-have, but meeting almost none of the requirements is usually a waste of time.
- **Not logging anything.** You apply twice, forget deadlines and mix up companies.
- **Sending several follow-ups in a row.** It looks pushy. One, at the right time, is enough.
- **Taking rejection personally.** Rejection usually reflects the number of candidates and the role's priorities, not your worth.
- **Stopping after a few negative answers.** Adjust your résumé and portfolio and keep going.

## Checklist

- Spreadsheet created with the columns above.
- Whole posting read before each application.
- Résumé tailored and saved with the role's name.
- Posting terms used only where they are true.
- Follow-up date logged.
- Spreadsheet reviewed every two weeks.
- One day off per week, without looking at openings.
